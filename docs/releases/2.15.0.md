# Bazis 2.15.0

## Changes

- **Distances from a point of the request.** A `PointField` in the LIST schema of a route
  sorts by the distance from a point given in `sort`:
  `sort=location__distance(37.62,55.75)` (`<longitude>,<latitude>` in degrees of WGS 84)
  puts the nearest objects first, `-location__distance(...)` the farthest first, the
  objects without a point last in both directions; other terms follow
  (`sort=location__distance(37.62,55.75),name`). The comma inside the parentheses does not
  separate the terms. Like any key of `sort`, the field must be in the LIST schema of the
  route: a point field the route hides, a field that is not a point, a path through a
  relation and a malformed or out-of-range point are 400 `ERR_FILTER` (`/query/sort`).
  The nearby list of a client is `?filter=location__near=37.62,55.75,10km&sort=
  location__distance(37.62,55.75)`: the point comes with the request, nothing is stored
  about the user.
- **`near` takes the unit of its distance.** `filter=location__near=<lon>,<lat>,<distance>`
  takes `500` (meters, as before), `500m` or `2.5km` (also a fraction); without a distance it
  is 100 m, and `location=<lon>,<lat>` is within 10 m, as before. The filter is now
  `ST_DWithin` on geography (the distance on the sphere): it uses the GiST index of a point
  field declared `geography=True` (Django creates it); a geometry point field (the
  default) is cast to geography on every row, without the index, as the distance function
  it used before. A malformed point or distance, a point out of range and a `near` with
  more than three values are 400 `ERR_FILTER`; some of them were a 500.
- `bazis.core.utils.geo`: `point_distance(field, point)` (meters on the sphere, the KNN
  operator `<->`: an `order_by` by it alone with a limit uses the index of a geography
  field),
  `point_within(field, point, meters)`, `parse_point('<lon>,<lat>')`,
  `parse_distance('2.5km')`, for the annotations, calculated fields and filters of a
  project; `point` is a GEOS point or an expression of a point (a `Subquery` of a point
  field).
- **The geometry attributes are written.** A create or an update that set a point (any
  geometry) answered 500: the GeoJSON object of the request was set on the field as it
  is. It is now a GEOS geometry, always of longitude, latitude in WGS 84 (RFC 7946; a field
  of another SRID stores it transformed). The request schemas check the GeoJSON against
  the field: 422 `ERR_VALIDATE` at the attribute for an object that is not a geometry, a
  geometry of another type than the field's (a polygon or a line into a `PointField`), a
  third coordinate on a 2D field (or none on a 3D one), a coordinate out of range
  (longitude -180..180, latitude -90..90) and any `crs` member (it named another
  coordinate system, which GEOS ignored: the data was stored distorted).
- **`manage.py bazis_messages`** keeps the gettext catalogs of the project
  (`<BASE_DIR>/locale` and the `locale` directories of its apps, never those of the
  installed packages), so that a project (an agent) does not fill the `.po` files with a
  script of its own:
  - `make` runs makemessages from BASE_DIR for the languages of `LANGUAGES` but English
    (`-l` to choose) without the obsolete entries, ignoring the hidden directories
    (`.venv`, `.scratch`) and `node_modules` anywhere, and `venv`, `frontend`, `static`,
    `media`, `build`, `dist` at the top of BASE_DIR unless they are Python packages (`-i`
    adds a pattern of makemessages), and creates `<BASE_DIR>/locale` first: without it
    makemessages writes the messages of the project into the first catalog of
    `LOCALE_PATHS`, the one of an installed package (`django.contrib.auth` in a new
    project), where msgmerge turns the entries of the package into obsolete ones;
  - `status` prints, by language, the `untranslated` and the `fuzzy` entries as JSON
    (`msgid`, `msgctxt`, `msgid_plural`; the guessed `msgstr` of a fuzzy one);
  - `apply <file.json>` sets the translations of a JSON file, `{"ru": {"<msgid>":
    "<msgstr>", "<plural msgid>": ["<form>", ...]}}` or a list of `{"msgctxt", "msgid",
    "msgstr"}`, drops their fuzzy flags, makes the header of a new catalog valid and
    compiles the catalogs (`msgfmt --check-format`); it reports the msgids that are not in
    the catalogs. Applying the file again changes nothing, and the next `make` keeps the
    catalog as `apply` wrote it;
  - `compile` compiles the catalogs only; `--check` fails while an entry stays
    untranslated or fuzzy, or a msgid of the file is unknown.
- The guide (`bazis/core/AGENTS.md`): the points and the distances with the nearby recipe,
  the catalogs of the project, the filter context of the calculated fields
  (`OuterRef('_user')`), and the signature of the custom routes (`http_post` with a body
  model, `RouteKind`, `set_item`).

## Upgrading

- New dependency: `polib` (the catalogs of `bazis_messages`). `bazis_messages` needs GNU
  gettext (`msgfmt`, `msgmerge`, `xgettext`), as `makemessages`.
- A distance of `near` on a geography field was measured on the spheroid; it is now on the
  sphere, as on a geometry field: an object at the edge of the radius (within 0.5 % of
  it) can enter or leave the result.
- `near` (and the point filter without a lookup) on a projected point field (not
  geodetic, such as `srid=3857`) measured in the units of the projection (Web Mercator
  meters, about 1.8 times the real distance at 56° of latitude); it is now meters on the
  sphere, as on the other point fields: the same number is a larger real radius there,
  and the filter returns more objects.
- A project that keeps coordinates in two `FloatField`s and computes the distance itself
  can move them to a `PointField(geography=True)` (a migration: add the field, fill it
  with `ST_SetSRID(ST_MakePoint(lon, lat), 4326)::geography`, drop the floats) and take
  the point of the client from the request with `near` and `sort`.
